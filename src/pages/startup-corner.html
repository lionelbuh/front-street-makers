City, county or state?

Most small businesses deal with three levels of government:

  • City Business license and home occupation rules, from the city where you operate (Temecula, Murrieta, Menifee and so on).
  • County Riverside County: Environmental Health for food businesses and food booths, and the County Clerk for business names.
  • State California: Cottage Food law (CDPH), the seller's permit and sales tax (CDTFA), and state business registrations.

If you live in an unincorporated area, outside city limits, ask Riverside County which rules apply to you.

Business licenses City

Temecula

The City of Temecula requires a business license for anyone doing business within city limits, including home-based businesses (Temecula Municipal Code 5.04.030). Licenses expire on January 31 each year. You can apply online or at the Permit Center at City Hall, 41000 Main Street.

  • City of Temecula: Business License Instructions
  • Questions: businesslicense@TemeculaCA.gov · (951) 693-3933

Murrieta

The City of Murrieta also requires a business license. Home-based businesses complete a Home Occupation Permit in addition to the license.

  • City of Murrieta: Business Licenses

Other cities

Menifee, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore and other cities run their own programs. Search your city's website for “business license”, or call city hall.

Home-based business rules City

Cities allow most quiet home businesses, with conditions that protect the neighborhood: how much of the home you use, customer visits, storage, signs and parking.

  • Temecula asks home businesses to submit a Home Occupation registration with the business license application. If you rent, the property owner's written authorization is required. The city's form also limits the business to a share of the home's floor area.
  • Murrieta limits home occupations to one customer or vendor at a time, between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., Monday through Saturday, with limited storage (Development Code 16.60.050).
  • Temecula: Business License Application, Home Occupation (PDF)
  • Murrieta Development Code 16.60.050: Home Occupations and Cottage Food Operations

Renting, or living with an HOA? Check your lease or HOA rules too: they can be stricter than the city's.

California Cottage Food State County

California's Cottage Food law lets you make certain lower-risk foods at home and sell them, such as many breads, cookies, jams, dried fruit, granola and candy. The state publishes the approved food list; foods that need refrigeration are generally not allowed.

  • Class A operations sell directly to customers: from home, at events, online with direct delivery.
  • Class B operations can also sell through permitted shops, cafés and restaurants (indirect sales).
  • Each class has a gross annual sales limit set in state law and adjusted for inflation each year. Check the current figures with CDPH.
  • In Riverside County you register (Class A) or get a permit (Class B) from Riverside County Environmental Health before you start. The county's 2025 application form listed fees of $205 (Class A) and $394 (Class B). Confirm the current amount before applying.
  • Operators complete an approved food processor course, and food labels must follow the Cottage Food labeling rules (see Packaging and labeling).
  • Food handler training: Riverside County requires a Riverside County Food Handler Certificate for people who handle food in permitted food facilities, including at events. The county's official online training and test is at riverside.statefoodsafety.com. Ask Environmental Health which training your operation needs.

Cooking full meals at home is a different program: Riverside County allows Microenterprise Home Kitchen Operations (MEHKO) with its own permit. Ask Environmental Health which one fits your food.

  • California Department of Public Health: Cottage Food Operations
  • Riverside University Health System – Environmental Health
  • Riverside County Code, Chapter 8.168: Cottage Food Operations
  • Riverside County Food Handler Certificate: official online training

Your city may also have rules for cottage food at home: Murrieta's are in the same code section as home occupations.

Seller's permit and sales tax State

If you sell physical goods in California, including handmade items sold from home, online or at craft fairs, you generally need a seller's permit from the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA), and you report sales tax on taxable sales. The permit itself is free.

  • Selling at one location for 90 days or less? There is a temporary seller's permit.
  • At events run by an organizer, you may need a sub-permit for that location even if you already hold a permit.
  • Some foods are not taxable, but you may still need a permit. CDTFA explains the exemptions.
  • CDTFA: Tax Guide for Home-Based Businesses
  • CDTFA Publication 107: Do You Need a California Seller's Permit?
  • CDTFA Publication 111: Sellers at swap meets, flea markets and special events

Business name County

If you do business under a name that isn't your own legal name (for example “Valley Sourdough” instead of “Alex Baker”), California requires a Fictitious Business Name statement, filed with the County Clerk and then published in a local newspaper. Riverside County's fee schedule (May 2025) lists $66 for one name and one owner.

  • Riverside County Assessor-County Clerk-Recorder: Fictitious Business Names

Forming an LLC or corporation is a separate state process with the California Secretary of State and the Franchise Tax Board. A tax professional can help you decide whether you need one.

Insurance

Homeowners' and renters' policies often exclude business activity, including inventory, equipment and customer visits. Common coverages for small makers:

  • General liability: injuries or damage linked to your business. Many markets and event organizers ask vendors for a certificate of insurance.
  • Product liability: claims related to something you made or sold. Important for food, body care and children's products.
  • A home-business rider on your existing policy, or a separate business policy for equipment and inventory.

Ask a licensed insurance agent what fits your activity. The California Department of Insurance explains how to check that an agent is licensed.

Markets, pop-ups and booth fees County

Booth fees are set by each market or event, and they vary widely. Before you sign up, ask:

  • What is the fee per day, per season, or as a share of sales? Is there a deposit or an application fee?
  • What is included: space size, tent, table, power, shade? Can I share a booth?
  • Which permits and insurance do you require from vendors?
  • How many people usually come, and are there other vendors selling the same thing?

Selling food at an event? Riverside County Environmental Health issues temporary food facility permits for community events, and food handlers need a Riverside County Food Handler Certificate. The organizer also needs approval, and booths serving unpackaged food have specific requirements. Apply well before the event.

  • Riverside County Environmental Health: Community events and temporary food facilities

Packaging and labeling State

  • Cottage foods must be labeled according to California's Cottage Food rules, including the statement that the food was made in a home kitchen, the product name, your registration or permit number, ingredients in order of weight and allergen information. See the CDPH Cottage Food page for the exact requirements.
  • Soap and body care: whether a product counts as soap or as a cosmetic depends on its ingredients and the claims you make, and the labeling rules differ. The FDA's cosmetics labeling guidance is the place to start.
  • Candles: add clear safety directions (never leave burning unattended, trim the wick, keep away from children and pets).
  • Everything: your business name and a way to contact you on every package helps customers come back.

Websites and online payments

You don't need an expensive website to start. Common, affordable paths:

  • A free social profile (Instagram, Facebook) with clear photos, prices and how to order.
  • A simple shop page from a payment provider. Square, Shopify Starter, Etsy and Stripe Payment Links all offer low-cost options; compare the transaction fees.
  • A small website with your own domain name when you're ready. Keep your menu, prices and pickup details easy to find on a phone.
  • Booking tools for services and classes, so customers can pick a time without messages back and forth.

Marketing and visibility

  • Good photos sell: natural daylight, a plain background, and one photo that shows scale (the product in a hand).
  • Say where and when: pickup days, delivery area and lead time for custom orders.
  • Collect regulars: a simple email list or a loyalty card at markets.
  • Work together: gift bundles with other local makers, or a shared booth.
