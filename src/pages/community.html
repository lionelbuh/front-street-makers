Ideas that help

Price for the market day, not just the materials

Add up ingredients or materials, packaging, your time at a fair hourly rate, booth fees and card fees, then the share of the day you spend not selling (setup, travel, slow hours). If the price feels high, offer a small “first taste” item rather than discounting your best work.

Photos that sell on a phone

Most neighbors will see your work on a small screen. Shoot near a window in daylight, keep the background plain, fill the frame with the product, and add one photo with a hand or a familiar object for scale. Three good photos beat twelve average ones.

Turn one sale into a regular

Put your name and how to order on every bag or label, mention when you'll be back, and offer a simple way to hear about new batches: a sign-up sheet, a QR code to your Instagram, or a punch card.
